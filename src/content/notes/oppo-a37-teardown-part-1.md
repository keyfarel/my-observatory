## Background

This is my father's old Oppo A37. It has been lying around unused for a very long time. Because it hasn't been charged or used in ages, the phone is completely dead and refuses to turn on. This is likely due to a deep-discharged battery or an issue with the power delivery line.

Out of curiosity, I decided to use it as a subject for a hardware experiment and began tearing it down.

## The Teardown Process

For this initial stage, my goal was simply to completely strip the phone down to its main components to inspect the internal condition. 

While taking apart the casing and internal parts, I noticed something interesting: **the motherboard appears to have been serviced before**. The signs were quite clear—there were chunks of dried, hardened glue in several spots that definitely weren't from the factory.

2. The Display (LCD)
3. The Frame

## Battery Check

To investigate the power issue, I checked the battery voltage using a multimeter. As suspected, it reads absolutely 0V. The battery is completely dead and unable to hold any charge.

*(Testing the battery voltage)*
![Battery Voltage Check](/images/notes/oppo-a37-phone-reparation/bateri-check-volt.webp)

## Next Steps

Since the battery is completely flat at 0V, my next move is to buy a replacement battery. Once the new battery arrives, I will test the motherboard again to see if it boots up or if there are other underlying issues causing it to stay dead.
